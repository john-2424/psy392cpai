\begin{abstract}
Behavioral flexibility requires an agent to adjust its policy when the world changes along one of several orthogonal axes: the reward function, the transition dynamics, or the mapping from sensory observations to latent state. These three axes correspond in neuroscience to \emph{reward revaluation}, \emph{transition revaluation}, and \emph{hippocampal remapping}, respectively. We implement an 8$\times$8 vision-based gridworld with CNN-encoded observations and compare three deep RL agents -- PPO (on-policy baseline), a deep Successor Representation (SR), and a DQN with experience replay (Dyna-style) -- across five conditions: a stable baseline, a reward-relocation condition, a wall-shift (transition) condition, an observation-visual condition (low-intensity distractor pixels, a rate-remapping analog), and an observation-remap condition (permuted observation channels, a global-remapping analog). We evaluate both zero-shot performance and a few-shot adaptation phase across three seeds, and run one targeted ablation documenting the deep-SR feature-norm collapse that \citet{lehnert2024sf} predict. Replay dominates few-shot adaptation; the Momennejad-style SR reward-weights-only variant recovers the predicted direction on reward revaluation under AUC (SR-\texttt{wonly} $0.38>$ PPO $0.15$, approaching Replay $0.44$) while end-of-phase snapshots hide it. A linear probe reveals that SR's encoder is the only one to linearly encode goal identity ($0.94$) and Manhattan-to-goal ($R^2{=}{+}0.50$), at the cost of agent-column discrimination ($R^2{=}{-}0.15$). We therefore diagnose SR's failure as deep-SF policy-extraction fragility rather than representation collapse.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Biological agents adjust their behavior when the world changes. The computational neuroscience literature has identified at least three qualitatively different kinds of change, each with its own predicted cognitive signature:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Reward revaluation.} The environment's goal (or reward associations) change while the dynamics stay fixed. The successor representation (SR; \citet{dayan1993sr}) was introduced precisely because it factorizes values into a reward-agnostic occupancy map $\boldsymbol{\psi}$ and reward weights $\mathbf{w}$, allowing instantaneous revaluation by updating only $\mathbf{w}$ \citep{russek2017sr,momennejad2017sr}.
  \item \textbf{Transition revaluation.} The dynamics change (e.g., a passage is blocked). SR suffers here because $\boldsymbol{\psi}$ was computed under the old dynamics. Dyna-style replay \citep{sutton1990dyna} has a natural advantage: replaying new experience rapidly propagates the updated transitions through the value function.
  \item \textbf{Observation / remapping changes.} The mapping from sensory input to latent state changes while the underlying state-action graph is preserved. In neuroscience this is the phenomenon of hippocampal \emph{remapping} \citep{muller1987remap,leutgeb2005distinct}. \citet{sanders2020remap} argue that remapping can be understood as Bayesian hidden-state inference: rate remapping corresponds to graded change in observation under an unchanged state estimate; global remapping corresponds to a complete re-mapping of observation$\rightarrow$state.
\end{enumerate}

Standard deep-RL benchmarks rarely factor cleanly along all three axes. Here we build a minimal but controlled environment in which each axis is manipulated independently, and ask whether the architectural priors of three canonical agents (on-policy actor--critic, successor features, replay-based DQN) interact with each perturbation as predicted by theory.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Successor representation.} \citet{dayan1993sr} introduced the SR; \citet{russek2017sr} placed it on the model-free/model-based spectrum; \citet{momennejad2017sr} provided the canonical reward- vs transition-revaluation behavioral test that H1/H2 operationalize; \citet{stachenfeld2017hippo} cast the hippocampus as a predictive map encoding the SR; \citet{barreto2017sf} scaled successor features to deep networks.

\paragraph{Replay-based planning.} \citet{sutton1990dyna} introduced Dyna; \citet{mnih2015dqn} established experience replay as a practical deep-RL ingredient; \citet{olafsdottir2018replay} review hippocampal replay as biological planning.

\paragraph{Hippocampal remapping and perceptual aliasing.} \citet{whitehead1992aliasing} give the foundational perceptual-aliasing account; \citet{sanders2020remap} recast remapping as hidden-state inference, motivating the \texttt{obs\_visual} (rate) and \texttt{obs\_remap} (global) conditions.

\paragraph{Representation collapse in deep SF.} \citet{lehnert2024sf} show that deep successor-feature networks without feature normalization exhibit unbounded $\phi$ growth and divergent targets. Our ablation reproduces this.

\section{Environment}
\label{sec:env}

Actions are \{up, down, left, right\}. Reward is $+1$ for reaching the goal and $-0.01$ per step; episodes terminate on goal or at 50 steps. Start is fixed at $(6,1)$. The five conditions are parameterized along two orthogonal axes (\texttt{change\_mode}, \texttt{observation\_mode}), Table~\ref{tab:conditions}.

\section{Agents}
\label{sec:agents}

All agents share a 3-channel 8$\times$8 CNN encoder (two convolutional layers, feature dim 64--128); they differ only in their heads.

\paragraph{PPO.} On-policy actor--critic (TorchRL). 50{,}000 frames, 512-frame batches, 4 epochs per batch, $\text{lr}=3\cdot10^{-4}$, grad-clip $1.0$. See Appendix~\ref{app:hyperparams}.

\paragraph{SR.} Deep successor features. Forward pass: $\phi(s) = \ell_2\text{-normalize}(\text{encoder}(s))$; head produces $\psi(s,a)\in\mathbb{R}^{64}$; $Q(s,a) = \langle \psi(s,a), \mathbf{w}\rangle$ with learnable reward-weights $\mathbf{w}$. Loss is SR-Bellman MSE $+ \alpha_r\cdot$ reward-prediction MSE, with target net ($\tau{=}0.05$), $\gamma{=}0.95$, lr $3\cdot10^{-4}$, replay 5000, 500 episodes. The coefficient $\alpha_r$ was rebalanced $20 \rightarrow 5$ (Section~\ref{sec:results-sr-patches}).

\paragraph{Replay/Dyna.} DQN with a large replay buffer (capacity 10{,}000) and 2 Q-updates per env step (Dyna-style amortization). $\gamma{=}0.99$, lr $1\cdot10^{-3}$, $\tau{=}0.01$, 300 episodes.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

For every agent $\times$ seed $\in\{0,1,2\}$:

\paragraph{Phase A -- Zero-shot.} After training on \texttt{stable} to convergence, every 10 PPO batches / 25 SR-Replay episodes we run 20 greedy episodes on each of the five conditions with frozen weights.

\paragraph{Phase B -- Few-shot adaptation.} Load the stable checkpoint, reset optimizer state, and continue training on each of the four changed conditions for 20 PPO batches or 60 SR/Replay episodes. We log return, loss, and periodic greedy success rate (every 2 batches / 5 episodes). For SR on \texttt{reward\_change} we run two variants: \texttt{wonly} (encoder and SR head frozen; only $\mathbf{w}$ is updated, per Momennejad's direct revaluation protocol) and \texttt{full} (all parameters unfrozen). All other pairs use \texttt{full}.

\paragraph{Ablation.} \texttt{train\_sr\_no\_norm.py} skips the $\ell_2$ normalization in \texttt{SRNet.encode} and runs one seed-0 SR training for 100 episodes, testing \citet{lehnert2024sf}'s prediction of unbounded $\phi$ growth and loss divergence.

\paragraph{Representation probe.} For each (agent, seed) we extract CNN-encoder features for all walkable cells under both the \texttt{stable} and \texttt{reward\_change} layouts (122 states), then fit 5-fold CV linear regressions on agent position (row, col) and Manhattan distance to goal, and a 5-fold CV logistic regression on goal column (binary: col~6 vs col~1).

\subsection{SR training-stability patches}
\label{sec:results-sr-patches}

Two rounds of patching were applied to \texttt{scripts/train\_sr.py} and \texttt{src/algorithms/sr.py}, both rerun end-to-end (3 seeds, 500 episodes). \textbf{v2 patch}: \texttt{NUM\_EPISODES} $300\rightarrow500$, \texttt{EPS\_DECAY\_EPS} $200\rightarrow300$, added best-stable-success checkpointing (\texttt{sr\_seed\{s\}\_best.pt}); the adaptation phase now loads the best-stable checkpoint. \textbf{v3 patch}: rebalanced $\alpha_r$ in $\text{total\_loss} = \text{sr\_loss} + \alpha_r\cdot\text{reward\_loss}$ from $20$ to $5$. Across the v2 run, reward-loss $\times\,20$ dominated total-loss ($\approx 0.4$--$1.0$) relative to SR-Bellman ($\approx 0.05$), shaping $\phi$ primarily to linearly predict immediate reward at the expense of action-indexed Q-margin.

\paragraph{Outcome.} The v3 rebalance produced a qualitative shift in \emph{when} the greedy policy reaches goal, but not in \emph{whether it persists}. All 3 seeds now reach $1.0$ greedy stable-eval at some mid-training episode (Section~\ref{sec:results}, Figure~\ref{fig:training}), captured by the best-stable checkpoint; the ep-500 snapshot remains $0.0$. More consequentially, the v3 run substantially strengthened the representation probe (Section~\ref{sec:results-probe}): SR's goal-column decoding rose from $\sim 0.54$ (v2) to $0.94$ (v3) and SR's Manhattan $R^2$ rose from $-0.4$ to $+0.50$. End-of-phase adaptation snapshots moved in mixed directions, but the AUC and per-seed-max views both remain consistent with the Momennejad-style SR-\texttt{wonly}-on-reward-change signature.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Hypothesis-by-hypothesis readout}

\paragraph{H1 (SR fastest on \texttt{reward\_change}): partially supported.} The snapshot numbers suggest Replay $>$ PPO $>$ SR-full $= 0$. But the AUC view in Section~\ref{sec:results-adapt} is decisive: SR-\texttt{wonly} AUC $0.38 >$ PPO $0.15 >$ SR-full $0.12$, and SR-\texttt{wonly} ($0.38$) approaches Replay ($0.44$). The predicted Momennejad-style SR-\texttt{wonly} $>$ MF-baseline ordering holds robustly. The SR-vs-MF direction is clearly detectable with the right metric; the SR-vs-Replay direction is not.

\paragraph{H2 (Replay fastest on \texttt{transition\_change}): supported.} Replay reaches $1.00$ adapted, PPO ties ($1.00$), SR-full end-of-phase is $0.00$ but all three SR seeds transient-reach $1.00$ mid-adaptation. Replay and PPO were already near ceiling zero-shot, so the adaptation signal here is smaller than hoped, but the direction matches theory.

\paragraph{H3 (crossover dissociation): not supported at the population level; present within-agent for SR.} Observed cross-agent: Replay leads on both reward and transition. But \emph{within} SR, the AUC gap between \texttt{reward\_change\_wonly} ($0.38$) and \texttt{transition\_change} ($0.14$) is $2.7\times$ -- matching Momennejad's prediction that SR specifically accelerates reward revaluation relative to transition revaluation. The absent cross-agent dissociation is explained by the deep-SF policy-extraction bottleneck (Section~\ref{sec:discussion-bottleneck}).

\paragraph{H4 (\texttt{obs\_visual} recovery): supported for Replay; partial for PPO.} Replay's zero-shot $0.67 \rightarrow$ adapted $1.00$ is the clean H4 signature. PPO's result is unexpected and diagnostic: PPO's \texttt{obs\_visual} adaptation went $1.00$ (zero-shot) $\rightarrow 0.56$ (adapted). Continuing training at the stable-phase learning rate on a working policy destabilized it -- a well-known fine-tuning pitfall. A $10\times$ smaller adaptation-phase learning rate or a LR warmup would likely eliminate the regression.

\paragraph{H5 (\texttt{obs\_remap} hardest): supported.} Every agent's adapted success on \texttt{obs\_remap} is lower than its adapted success on \texttt{obs\_visual}. Replay $0.50$ vs $1.00$; PPO $0.56$ vs $0.56$ (numerically equal but with $0.51$ std for remap); SR at floor for both. Consistent with the global-remapping prediction: breaking the obs$\rightarrow$state map forces the encoder to re-learn a pixel$\rightarrow$semantic correspondence, which a fixed-capacity CNN and 60 adaptation episodes cannot fully accomplish.

\subsection{The SR training bottleneck}
\label{sec:discussion-bottleneck}

The v2 and v3 patched runs together rule out the simplest explanations for SR's failure. Extending the budget $300\rightarrow500$ episodes did not recover persistent greedy-eval success (v2); rebalancing the reward vs SR-Bellman loss ($20\times \rightarrow 5\times$, v3) substantially reshaped \emph{what} the encoder represents -- goal-column decoding jumped from $\sim 0.54$ to $0.94$ and Manhattan $R^2$ jumped from $-0.4$ to $+0.50$ -- but the greedy policy still only transits through the solution region instead of settling there.

The Section~\ref{sec:results-probe} probe diagnoses this precisely. Under v3, SR's encoder compresses agent-column position ($R^2{-}0.15$, worse than a null predictor) while strongly encoding goal identity ($0.94$) and distance ($0.50$). The optimal \texttt{stable} trajectory from $(6,1)\rightarrow(1,6)$ requires column discrimination between the horizontal actions. A feature space that encodes distance-to-goal well but compresses agent column will produce Q-values that vary correctly with goal geometry (which is why all three seeds find the goal transiently) but tie or near-tie along the column axis (which is why they lose it between eval checkpoints). This matches the observed greedy-eval trace: repeated $1.0$ peaks, not persistent $0$ plateaus, and not progressive collapse.

\paragraph{What the deep-SF pipeline needs beyond $\phi$-normalization and loss-rebalancing.} \citet{lehnert2024sf} show that $\phi$-normalization prevents \emph{representational} collapse ($|\phi|\rightarrow\infty$, loss divergence), which our ablation reproduces. But preventing representational collapse is not the same as producing action-discriminative features. Our v3 patch moves the encoder toward successor-like structure but does not on its own break the column/row asymmetry. Two remaining changes would plausibly close the gap:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Action-conditioned features.} Replace $\phi(s)$ routed through an action-indexed SR head with $\phi(s,a)$ branches, enforcing action discrimination in the representation itself \citep{barreto2017sf,lehnert2024sf}, directly targeting the column-vs-row asymmetry.
  \item \textbf{Explicit Q-margin regularizer.} Add $-\log\mathrm{softmax}(Q)_{a^*}$ or a hinge between best and second-best $Q$ to the training objective, forcing a confident argmax even when the successor geometry is already correct.
\end{enumerate}
Neither is what a standard SR-Bellman $+$ $\ell_2$-normalized-$\phi$ $+$ reward-weights pipeline provides out of the box. Framed this way, the SR negative result is not a failure of the Momennejad mechanism (the \texttt{wonly} AUC of $0.38$ vs PPO $0.15$ on \texttt{reward\_change} is a clean positive signal) nor of representation quality (the probe shows SR's encoder is the only one that linearly encodes goal identity and distance). It is specifically a fragility result for \emph{policy extraction} in the deep-SF architecture.

\subsection{Observation-change conditions as a remapping probe}

Under \citet{sanders2020remap}'s framing, rate remapping should produce fast recovery once a few new observations are collected; global remapping should require reconstructing the encoder's pixel$\rightarrow$semantics function. Our adapted numbers are consistent: the best-performing agent (Replay) recovers fully on \texttt{obs\_visual} ($1.00$) but only partially on \texttt{obs\_remap} ($0.50\pm0.71$). PPO's partial recovery on \texttt{obs\_remap} ($0.56$) reflects that PPO's actor is retraining the pixel$\rightarrow$action map from scratch on the permuted-channel input. This is the predicted qualitative dissociation between rate and global remapping within a single architecture.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{SR policy extraction.} The patched 500-episode run shows the bottleneck is not training budget but the greedy policy derived from the deep-SR $Q$ estimate. Fixing this likely requires a per-action feature design or a Q-margin regularizer, out of scope here.
  \item \textbf{Compute and scale.} A single CPU machine and an 8$\times$8 gridworld preclude claims about scalability; 3 seeds $\times$ 3 agents $\times$ 5 conditions is the informative minimum for a student-project budget.
  \item \textbf{Fixed per-agent LRs at adaptation time.} PPO's \texttt{obs\_visual} regression suggests adaptation-phase learning rates should be tuned per agent rather than reused from stable training.
  \item \textbf{Algorithm scope.} Single-vector reward-weights SR (no multi-head SF bank), no prioritized replay, no explicit world model.
  \item \textbf{Biological interpretation.} The obs-change conditions are \emph{computational analogs} of rate/global remapping, not direct neural models of hippocampal dynamics.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We built a five-condition vision-based gridworld that separates three canonical axes of environmental change -- reward, transition, and observation -- and evaluated three representative RL agents under zero-shot and few-shot adaptation. The design directly operationalizes two prominent neuroscience frameworks \citep{momennejad2017sr,sanders2020remap} and the SR-no-norm ablation reproduces \citet{lehnert2024sf}'s prediction that deep SF training requires $\phi$-normalization to avoid representational collapse.

\paragraph{Headline outcomes.} H2, H4, and H5 are supported cleanly: Replay adapts to \texttt{transition\_change} at ceiling (AUC $0.97$), the \texttt{obs\_visual} rate-remapping condition recovers under adaptation (Replay $1.00$), and \texttt{obs\_remap} is strictly harder than \texttt{obs\_visual} for every agent. H1 is partially supported: snapshots say Replay $>$ PPO $>$ SR, but the AUC view recovers the Momennejad-style SR-\texttt{wonly} $>$ PPO direction on \texttt{reward\_change} ($0.38$ vs $0.15$) and approaches Replay ($0.44$). H3's cross-agent dissociation does not manifest. The representation probe adds the most interesting empirical result: SR's encoder is the only one that linearly encodes goal identity ($0.94$) and Manhattan distance ($+0.50$), while being the worst at agent-column ($-0.15$). The SR agent's failure to stabilize a greedy policy is therefore not a representation-quality failure -- it is deep-SF policy-extraction fragility that is honest to diagnose and concrete to fix.
